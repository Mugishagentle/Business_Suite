# Notification Engine Specification

Version: 1.0

Status: Draft

Module: Notification Engine

---

# 1. Purpose

The Notification Engine is a shared platform service responsible for delivering communications generated by business events across the Business Suite.

It provides centralized notification generation, routing, delivery, tracking, and auditing for all platform services and business modules.

---

# 2. Core Principle

Business modules do not send notifications directly.

Business modules publish business events.

The Notification Engine determines:

- Whether a notification should be sent.
- Who should receive it.
- Which channels should be used.
- Which template should be applied.
- When the notification should be delivered.

---

# 3. Objectives

The Notification Engine aims to:

- Centralize notification delivery.
- Support multiple delivery channels.
- Support reusable notification templates.
- Support tenant-specific branding.
- Support notification preferences.
- Support delivery tracking.
- Support retry mechanisms.
- Support audit history.
- Support future integrations.

---

# 4. Scope

The Notification Engine includes:

- Business Events
- Notification Rules
- Notification Templates
- Recipient Resolution
- Channel Selection
- Message Generation
- Delivery Queue
- Delivery Tracking
- User Preferences
- Notification History

The engine acts as the communication layer for the Business Suite.

---

# 5. Out of Scope

The Notification Engine does not:

- Execute business workflows.
- Manage approval processes.
- Store business data.
- Replace business logic.

Business modules remain responsible for raising business events.

The Notification Engine is responsible for communication only.

---

# 6. Core Concepts

The Notification Engine is built around the following concepts:

- Business Events
- Notification Rules
- Notification Channels
- Notification Templates
- Recipients
- Delivery Queue
- Delivery Attempts
- User Preferences
- Notification History

---

# 7. Business Event

A Business Event is an occurrence within the platform that may trigger a notification.

Examples:

- User Invited
- Password Reset Requested
- Trial Expiring
- Workflow Approval Assigned
- Workflow Approved
- Invoice Created
- Payment Received
- Low Stock Detected
- Leave Approved

Business modules publish events.

The Notification Engine listens and decides whether to send notifications.

---

# 8. Notification Rule

A Notification Rule defines when a notification should be sent.

A rule may define:

- Event Code
- Tenant
- Module
- Recipient Type
- Channel
- Template
- Status

Example:

```text
Event

Workflow Approval Assigned

↓

Send In-App Notification

↓

Send Email

↓

Recipient: Assigned Approver
```

Rules may be global or tenant-specific.

---

# 9. Notification Channel

A Notification Channel defines how a notification is delivered.

Supported Version 1 channels:

- In-App
- Email
- SMS

Future channels:

- Push Notification
- WhatsApp
- Microsoft Teams
- Slack
- Webhook

Channels should be configurable.

Business modules must never send directly through a channel provider.

---

# 10. Notification Template

A Notification Template defines the content of a notification.

Templates may include:

- Subject
- Message Body
- Channel
- Language
- Variables
- Branding

Example variables:

```text
{{user_name}}
{{company_name}}
{{document_number}}
{{approval_link}}
{{expiry_date}}
```

Templates should be reusable and tenant-aware.

---

# 11. Recipient

A Recipient is the user or contact who receives a notification.

Recipients may be resolved by:

- Specific User
- Role
- Permission
- Workflow Approver
- Document Owner
- Record Owner
- Department Head
- Supervisor
- External Email
- External Phone Number

Recipient resolution must be dynamic.

---

# 12. Delivery Queue

Notifications should be queued before delivery.

Queueing allows:

- Retry
- Background processing
- Failure tracking
- Rate limiting
- Provider failover in future

The user interface should not freeze while notifications are being delivered.

---

# 13. Delivery Attempt

A Delivery Attempt records each attempt to send a notification.

Each attempt should capture:

- Channel
- Provider
- Status
- Error Message
- Attempt Number
- Sent Date
- Delivered Date

This supports troubleshooting and auditability.

---

# 14. User Preferences

Users may configure how they receive notifications where allowed.

Examples:

- Email enabled
- SMS enabled
- In-App enabled
- Marketing notifications disabled
- Security notifications always enabled

Security-related notifications may override user preferences.

---

# 15. Notification History

Every generated notification should be recorded.

History should show:

- Event
- Recipient
- Channel
- Template
- Status
- Created Date
- Delivered Date

Notification history is useful for audit, troubleshooting, and support.

---

# 16. Notification Lifecycle

Every notification follows the lifecycle below.

```text
Business Event Published

↓

Notification Rule Matched

↓

Recipients Resolved

↓

Template Selected

↓

Message Generated

↓

Notification Queued

↓

Delivery Attempted

↓

Delivered / Failed

↓

History Recorded

```

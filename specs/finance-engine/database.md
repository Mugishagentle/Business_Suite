# Finance Engine

## DATABASE.md

---

# 1. Database Overview

The Finance Engine database provides the persistent financial storage layer for the Business Suite platform.

It stores all financial information generated by business modules while maintaining strict tenant isolation, financial integrity, auditability, and enterprise scalability.

The Finance Engine database is designed using Domain-Driven Design (DDD) principles.

Financial data is organized into logical domains rather than a single collection of unrelated tables.

This architecture improves:

- Maintainability
- Performance
- Security
- Scalability
- Future extensibility

---

# 2. Database Architecture

The Finance database is organized into the following domains.

```text
Finance Database

│
├── Finance Core
│
├── General Ledger
│
├── Financial Configuration
│
├── Accounts Receivable
│
├── Accounts Payable
│
├── Cash Management
│
├── Banking
│
├── Reconciliation
│
└── Financial Reporting
```

Each domain owns its own entities.

Domains communicate through service contracts rather than direct table manipulation.

---

# 3. Multi-Tenant Database Architecture

The Finance Engine follows the Business Suite tenant hierarchy.

```text
Platform

      │

Tenant

      │

Company

      │

Branch

      │

Finance Data
```

Every Finance entity belongs to exactly one Tenant.

Most Finance entities also belong to one Company.

Operational transactions may additionally belong to one Branch.

No Finance record may exist outside this hierarchy.

---

# 4. Tenant Data Isolation

Tenant isolation is enforced at every layer of the platform.

Isolation mechanisms include:

- PostgreSQL Row Level Security (RLS)
- Active Tenant Context
- Authorization Engine
- Finance Validation Layer
- Service Layer Validation

Every database query automatically filters data using the active Tenant.

Cross-tenant access is never permitted.

---

# 5. Database Design Principles

The Finance database follows several core principles.

## Single Source of Truth

Financial information is stored once.

Duplicate financial records are avoided.

---

## Immutable Financial History

Posted financial records are never modified.

Corrections create new financial transactions.

---

## Normalized Master Data

Reference information is normalized.

Examples include:

- Accounts
- Tax Codes
- Currencies
- Payment Methods

---

## Transactional Consistency

Every posting operation executes inside a database transaction.

Either the complete posting succeeds or the entire transaction is rolled back.

---

## Auditability

Every financial entity supports complete historical auditing.

---

## Extensibility

Future financial capabilities can be introduced without redesigning the database.

---

# 6. Entity Ownership

The Finance Engine owns only financial entities.

It does not own operational business data.

Finance-owned entities include:

- Accounts
- Journals
- Journal Lines
- Ledger Entries
- Fiscal Years
- Accounting Periods
- Taxes
- Exchange Rates
- Customer Financial Accounts
- Supplier Financial Accounts
- Cash Accounts
- Bank Accounts

Business modules retain ownership of:

- Customers
- Suppliers
- Inventory
- Products
- Employees
- Assets
- Projects

The Finance Engine references these entities through immutable identifiers rather than duplicating business data.

---

# 7. Domain Organization

The Finance database is divided into independent domains.

| Domain                  | Responsibility                                   |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Finance Core            | Shared finance configuration and system settings |
| General Ledger          | Accounting foundation and journals               |
| Financial Configuration | Taxes, currencies, payment methods               |
| Accounts Receivable     | Customer financial records                       |
| Accounts Payable        | Supplier financial records                       |
| Cash Management         | Cash transactions and balances                   |
| Banking                 | Bank accounts and banking transactions           |
| Reconciliation          | Matching and reconciliation processes            |
| Financial Reporting     | Reporting views and summarized datasets          |

Each domain maintains clear ownership boundaries and exposes its functionality through the Finance Service Layer.

# 14. General Ledger Domain

The General Ledger Domain is the foundation of the Finance Engine.

Every financial transaction generated anywhere in Business Suite ultimately posts to the General Ledger.

The General Ledger is the official book of accounts for each Company.

It maintains the complete financial history required to produce all financial statements.

---

## General Ledger Architecture

```text
General Ledger

├── Chart of Accounts

├── Journal Lines
├── Ledger Entries
├── Posting Rules
└── Posting Batches
```

Every accounting operation begins and ends within the General Ledger Domain.

The Reconciliation Domain is responsible for ensuring that financial records maintained within the Finance Engine accurately match external and internal financial sources.

It provides centralized reconciliation services for all financial sub-ledgers and the General Ledger.

The Reconciliation Domain does not create accounting transactions.

Instead, it validates, matches, identifies differences, and where necessary generates reconciliation adjustments through the Posting Engine.

# 92. Data Integrity Rules

The Finance Engine enforces strict data integrity across all entities.

## General Rules

- Every entity belongs to a Tenant.
- Every financial entity belongs to a Company.
- Operational entities may belong to a Branch.
- Every Journal must balance.
- Every Ledger Entry originates from a Journal Line.
- Posted records are immutable.
- Financial history is never physically deleted.

---

## Referential Integrity

The database enforces foreign key relationships between all financial entities.

Examples include:

- Journal → Journal Type
- Journal → Accounting Period
- Journal Line → Journal
- Journal Line → Account
- Ledger Entry → Journal Line
- Receipt → Customer Financial Account
- Payment → Supplier Financial Account
- Bank Transaction → Bank Account
- Cash Transaction → Cash Account

Broken financial references are not permitted.

---
